Hands-free scrolling on Android: read long pages without touching the screen

Long articles, webtoons, chat logs and recipes mean swiping again and again. Here is how to scroll an Android screen without touching it.

1. Voice Access (Google)

Google's free Voice Access app lets you say “scroll down” or “scroll up” in the languages it supports. It helps when your hands are busy and the room is quiet enough to talk.

2. Auto-scroll inside some reading apps

Some e-book and comic apps have their own auto-scroll. It works only inside that app.

3. Auto Scroll (in the app you are reading)

A floating button scrolls the app you are reading. Touch the screen and it pauses; lift your finger and it continues after 0.5, 1.2 or 3 seconds, or only when you tap the button. It chains short swipes instead of jumping a page, stays above the gesture bar and works in full-screen apps. Long-press the button to turn everything off.

Five settings: speed, resume delay, button size, button opacity and direction. No ads, no account, no analytics. The accessibility service sends the scroll gestures and notices when you scroll yourself or a keyboard opens, so it can pause. The app does not read, store or send the text or images on your screen.

Tips

  • Webtoons: a slow speed with a 1.2-second resume lets you stop on a panel and keep going.
  • Recipes: choose to resume only on a button tap, so the page waits while you cook.
